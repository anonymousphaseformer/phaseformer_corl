\textbf{Spread in the training starts, not variation along the path, is what lets a deterministic policy split between the two routes.} Four MLP policies (colour; the score arms use $\lambda = 0.5$; the phase-conditioned ones are in bold) are each trained on 1024 demonstrations from four datasets that differ in one factor (named over each block). \textbf{fixed}: two mirror-image trajectories (left and right) from one exact start pose, repeated. \textbf{+via}: the same start with 0.3\,m via-point jitter, so the demonstrations vary mid-path. \textbf{+start}: start-pose noise (8\,cm, 0.05\,rad) and no via jitter. \textbf{C}: the full scenario, with start noise, via jitter, goal-position noise and a free goal heading. \textbf{Left}: $\mathrm{MMD}^2 \times 10^3$ to the set's held-out demonstrations (log scale). \textbf{Right}: success (within 0.6\,m of the goal with no collision). Each dot is one of 10 seeds, the bar is their mean, and the violin is a kernel density estimate over the seeds; a flat lens marks a policy whose seeds all scored the same. $\dagger$ marks $p < 0.05$ against MLP+phase+score trained on the same set (Welch's $t$-test over seeds). With a single start (fixed, +via), every seed of every policy collides (0\% success), whether or not there is via jitter. Start noise alone restores success. On +start, the score-regularized policies reach 95--97\% with little variation across seeds; the unregularized MLP and MLP+phase reach 60\% and 79\% on average, with individual seeds ranging from 17\% to 100\%. C shows the same ordering (63 / 87 / 72 / 89\%). MMD is measured against each set's own demonstrations, so it is not comparable across sets.
